- [x] **Stepped lessons — teach one idea, practise it, then the next (NEW lesson shape).** A lesson can now declare **`steps`** instead of relying on a single `explanation` screen: each step teaches one point and immediately practises *just that point*, with a progress bar across the top, a **← Previous idea** button, and a **recap** page collecting everything before the graded test. **The test is deliberately unchanged** — same 8 questions, same 75%, still a real gate; Danny's call was that the test should stay exactly as hard as it was. Lessons that don't declare `steps` keep the original explain → practice → test flow with no change at all (verified against `genitive`). Why it exists: **Prefixes That Change Meaning** packed ~15 facts and two tables onto one screen and then tested all of it at once, with no checkpoint in between — so the first signal that an idea hadn't landed was the graded score. (Danny hit exactly this: 50% on that test, against 87–100% on every other lesson he's passed.)
  - **First unit rebuilt: Prefixes That Change Meaning — 7 ideas, 17 practice questions** (was 1 screen, 8 practice). The ideas: (1) a meaning prefix builds a brand-new verb, and it comes out perfective; (2) rebuilding the imperfective with **-ыва- / -ива-**; (3) one root, six verbs — the писа́ть family; (4) direction prefixes I — при- / у- / в- / вы-; (5) direction prefixes II — под- and пере-, the two you already met on писа́ть; (6) amount-of-action prefixes — по- / за- / до-; (7) the catch — the same prefix can be empty on one verb and meaningful on another (постро́ить vs поговори́ть). Practice is **weighted toward ideas 4–6**, because six of the eight graded questions come from those. The legacy `explanation` and `practice` fields are kept as a fallback but are unused while `steps` is present.
  - **New test suite: `tests/lesson-invariants.mjs`** (1,503 assertions) — extracts the real `LESSONS` array out of the HTML and checks both lesson shapes: unique ids, well-formed questions (answer index in range, unique non-blank options), every practice item carries a `why`, every step teaches something and practises it, a stepped lesson has a recap, the pass mark is reachable, and no lesson half-declares the shape (a `recap` without `steps`). Headless-verified end-to-end besides: all 7 steps → 17 practice questions with feedback → recap → test intro → 8/8 pass → lesson marked done → 12 grammar-review items seeded; back/forward navigation between ideas; and the classic flow proven unchanged. Zero console errors.

  - **Story authoring principles (set 2026-07-23, prompted by the «Она светлая» snag).** Every story — built by any future session — must follow these so content never drifts into Duolingo-style weirdness or ambiguous glosses:
    1. **Natural Russian, real scenes.** Sentences a real person would actually say, in a coherent little scene with a through-line — never disconnected drill lines. The lesson's grammar appears *because the scene needs it*, not by contorting a sentence to cram the structure in.
    2. **Level-appropriate & plausible.** Vocabulary within the learner's cumulative reach; realistic, culturally real situations (dachas, trains, family visits) — no surreal filler ("the turtle drinks your coffee").
    3. **Unambiguous English.** The gloss and translation pick the meaning that fits *this* context; avoid double-meaning English words (e.g. "bright" = clever vs. well-lit — for a room, say "light / well-lit").
    4. **Grammatical gender is a feature — surface it, don't hide it.** он/она/оно pointing at an *object* must be translated as **"it," not "he / she,"** and the gloss should flag *why* (e.g. «она» because комната is grammatically feminine). This is worth *teaching*, not smoothing over — the reader's takeaway from «Она светлая» was precisely that «она» = "it (feminine noun)" is a genuinely useful thing to learn. Make it clear, not confusing.
    5. **Meaning first, grammar incidental.** The featured grammar is showcased through natural use; comprehension questions check real understanding plus the target point.

### A2→B1 "bridge" lessons (candidates — flagged 2026-07-23)
Source: Gemini flagged these gaps in the current grammar spine; reviewed, confirmed as genuine, and priority-ranked here. **None built yet** — the call was to note, not build. Each would follow the standard lesson format (explanation + 8 practice + 8 test + a 12-item grammar-review pool) and add a few questions to the relevant exam bank. Placement when built: the A2→B1 boundary — late-A2 or early-B1 `BAND_LESSONS` entries (before Reflexive Verbs / Verbs of Motion).

*High value — build first:*
- [ ] **Relative clauses with кото́рый** — "the man who… / the book that…"; кото́рый agrees in gender & number with the noun it refers to, but takes its **case from its own clause**. The single biggest gap — you can't get past simple sentences without it.
- [ ] **Reflexive possessive свой** — "his-own / her-own" vs. someone else's (его́ vs. свой); a constant error source. NB: this is **distinct** from the existing "Reflexive Verbs" (-ся) lesson — свой is about possession, not verbs.
- [ ] **Short-form adjectives & predicatives** — до́лжен, ну́жен, рад, гото́в, бо́лен; short forms used in the predicate. Very high frequency (до́лжен = "have to").
- [ ] **Negative pronouns + double negation** — никто́, ничто́/ничего́, никогда́, нигде́ … always with **не** on the verb (Russian requires the double negative; "nobody knows nothing" is correct here).

*Useful, more specialized:*
- [ ] **Linking ideas: что́бы / ли / reported speech** — что́бы + past for purpose & wishes; ли as the yes/no-question particle; reported speech (Russian has **no tense backshift**, so it's simpler than English). Clusters neatly into one lesson.
- [ ] **Verbs of position** — стоя́ть/лежа́ть/сиде́ть (being in a position) vs. ста́вить/класть/ве́шать (putting into one).

*Lower priority — nuance/polish:*
- [ ] **Emphasis particles же, ведь, да́же** — discourse flavor; makes speech sound natural rather than teaching new structure. (ли is essential but fits better inside the что́бы lesson above.)

*Vocabulary note (correcting the "1,500 is too few" concern):* no new words are needed for this — the deck already holds **2,003 words**. The "B1 1,500" figure is only the **exam-unlock gate**, not the size of the material, so Gemini's 2,000–2,500 target is effectively already available; the words just aren't all switched on in the deck picker. If more depth is wanted later, the move is a **B2 tier/goal**, not filling toward 1,500.

## Polish / nice-to-haves

- [x] **Fixed: Home no longer promises "new words" that aren't there.** When every word in the *active* decks had already been introduced, Home's "Next up" and the banner still advertised "N new words" (computed as `NEW_PER_DAY - newDone`, i.e. leftover daily allowance) while the drill correctly served nothing — so tapping "Start drill" landed on the empty/done screen. Now the new-word count is capped by real availability (`availNewCount()` = un-introduced words in active decks), used in both `owedToday()` and `updateBanner()`. And when active decks are exhausted but other decks still hold unseen words (`anyDeckHasUnseen()`), the recommender switches to **"You've learned every word in your active decks — Choose a deck →"** (opens the deck picker) instead of pointing at an empty drill. Verified against the live save: exhausted state shows the deck nudge with the banner hidden; turning on an A2 deck restores the 8 new words.
- [x] **Met vs known (honest measure).** The word goals count words *met* (exposure — seen at least once), which overstates real ability. Home now shows **"Words met"** and **"Words known"** side by side (known = answered right across several spaced reviews — the retained count), with hover explanations; the path and "Next up" recommender now read `X / target met · Y known`. The exam-unlock gate deliberately stays on *met* (Danny's call), so the honest retention gap is *visible* without making progression harder. `knownCount()` already existed and drives the Level band — this just surfaces it next to "met" everywhere the goal is shown.
